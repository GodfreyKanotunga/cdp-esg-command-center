Key Finding

High CDP scorers were already cutting a bit more — but after the score is released, there is no clear extra cut.

Same period (the year the firm was being scored): each grade higher goes with — points of change in Scope 1+2 (p = —). After the score (the year after it was released): — points (p = —), 95% CI — to —. So the score describes firms that are cutting; we can't show it predicts further cuts. The after-score sample is smaller, so a small effect can't be ruled out either. Association only, not cause.

Limits●
  • Association only. Firms already planning cuts may also score well.
  • After-score sample is smaller (2 score years), so it can miss a small effect.
  • Sample leans to larger, well-organised reporters.
  • Changes can come from selling or buying business units, not real cuts.
  • 2020 → 2021 includes COVID effects.
  • Scope 2 is location-based. Scope 3 is not used.
Method●
  • Same period: score year t = 2018 – 2020, change from report t+1. After score: t = 2018 – 2019, change from report t+2. Both use reporting year vs "Past year 1" (C6.1 + C6.3) inside one report.
  • Kept when the two periods end 335 – 395 days apart and both totals are above zero.
  • Change = log(current / previous), shown as %. Trimmed at 1st / 99th percentile, e.g. after score — to —.
  • Bands: A / A-, B / B-, C / D (incl. C-, D-). Grade step: A = 8 … D- = 1.
  • "Two reports": reporting-year total in report t+1 vs report t (same period, bigger sample, restatement noise).
