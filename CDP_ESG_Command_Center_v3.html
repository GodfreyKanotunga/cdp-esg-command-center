Key Finding

Governance practices explain 31% of variation in CDP scores — emissions size does not.

Across 16,957 firm-years (108 countries, 2018–2021), OLS regression with HC3 robust standard errors shows that seven binary governance indicators collectively predict CDP disclosure quality (R² = 0.31, n = 8,331). Once governance is controlled, log-transformed emissions become statistically insignificant (p = 0.614), consistent with stakeholder and legitimacy theory: larger emitters score higher in the bivariate (+0.13 correlation) because investor scrutiny forces disclosure investment — not because scale inherently drives quality.

Legitimacy / Stakeholder Theory
Key insight: β(log emissions) = −0.004, p = 0.614 once governance controlled. The bivariate correlation (+0.13) is explained by scrutiny: large emitters face stakeholder pressure and invest more in disclosure infrastructure.

Data Sources

CDP Climate Change Dataset 2018–2021 · 16,957 firm-years · 108 countries · 61 sectors · Validated against original flat/non-uniform CDP questionnaire exports

Methodology

OLS regression with HC3 robust SE · Random Forest (200 trees, max depth 8) · CART (depth 5) · 5-fold CV · Log1p transform for emissions · Binary governance flags from C1–C12 questions

Limitations

OLS applied to 7-level ordinal outcome (ordered logit recommended) · 50.9% unscored — governance rates computed on scored subsample only · Cross-sectional: causal claims not established
